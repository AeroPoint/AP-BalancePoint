Getting a CSV from your bank

  1. Sign in to your bank's website and open the checking account or card.
  2. On the transactions list, choose the download or export option.
  3. Pick a date range and the CSV format.
  4. Upload the file here and choose the matching account.

Files from other banks work too, as long as they have a date column, a description or name column, and an amount (or debit and credit) column.

Getting your Amazon orders

Your whole history: Amazon's own export

  1. On amazon.com, open Account, then Request Your Data (under Data and Privacy).
  2. Choose Your Orders and submit, then confirm with the link Amazon emails you.
  3. In a few hours to a few days Amazon emails a download link. Download the ZIP.
  4. Upload it here. A household with two Amazon accounts uploads both.

Each month, quicker: the Order History Reporter extension

  1. Install the free, open-source Amazon Order History Reporter extension for Chrome.
  2. Sign in to amazon.com and go to Returns & Orders (Your Orders): the extension doesn't work from Amazon's home page.
  3. Open the extension there, choose the orders table for the last month or two, and download its CSV.
  4. Do the same with its items table, for the same months.
  5. Upload the orders CSV here first, then the items CSV. Two Amazon accounts: do both.

The orders CSV has each order's real total, which equals its bank charge, so it's what matches a charge to an order. The items CSV then shares that total out among the order's items by price (close, not exact: check the Split form before saving). An order charged in parts as it shipped won't match. If Amazon shows a captcha, solve it in an Amazon tab and run the extension again, or pick fewer months.

Turn on “Sort by hand” and “Can be split” for Amazon in the Merchant dictionary, so its charges wait in Categorize with their items.
