How bumpy the ride is

Your investments together ({{ x.total|money(false) }} today) grow about {{ '%.1f'|format(x.rate) }}% a year, swinging about ±{{ '%.0f'|format(x.vol) }}% in a typical year.

  • Falls along the way: in a typical future, at some point in the {{ '%.0f'|format(p.span) if p.span >= 1 else 'under 1' }} year{{ 's' if p.span != 1 }} your investments drop {{ '%.0f'|format(x.drop_typical * 100) }}% from their high before recovering (about {{ (x.drop_typical * x.total)|money(false) }} on today's balance). How this works

  • Each month, every account you include grows at its rate. Accounts that swing (investments) also get a random market move: one move for all of them, scaled by each account's swings, because markets tend to fall together. Paycheck 401(k) money goes in, loans pay down on their schedules{% if p.with_plan %}, and the Plan's month happens: its surplus or shortfall lands in {{ names.get(p.plan_account, 'the spending account') }}, the backups top it up as on the Plan page, and any savings goal goes into its account. The Plan's changes run through its last month ({{ p.plan_end|month_label }}); after that the planned month carries on as it is now (income, spending and any savings goal), without the changes. Plan further ahead on the Plan page to include later changes{% endif %}.
  • {{ p.runs }} futures are played out this way, each with different market luck (the same set every time you open the page, so numbers only change when your data or settings do). The expected path is the same month-by-month math with no luck at all.
  • Rates come from an account's own history once it has 3 years of balances, otherwise from a typical figure for its kind. Change any of them below. "Swings" is volatility: how far a year usually lands from the average (about 15 for a stock fund, 0 for cash).
  • What would it take reruns those same futures with a monthly amount added, split across the accounts you pick, and finds the smallest amount that reaches the goal on the expected path, or in 3 of 4 or 9 of 10 futures. A goal in today's dollars is raised by inflation ({{ p.inflation }}% a year) to the dollars of that date first.
  • It's an estimate: real markets have streaks and crashes bigger than this model's, and rates of the past don't promise the future. Use it to compare choices, not as a promise.
